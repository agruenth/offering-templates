Details

The problem

AI agents face a qualitatively different threat surface from traditional software. Conventional penetration testing looks for injection into SQL queries, APIs, and shell commands. AI agents are vulnerable to injection into the prompt itself — through user input, through documents the agent retrieves, and through the outputs of tools the agent calls. An attacker who can influence any text the agent reads can potentially hijack its behaviour, exfiltrate data it has accessed, or escalate its privileges to call tools outside its intended scope.

Across enterprise red-team exercises, we consistently find 3–7 exploitable vulnerabilities in agents that passed internal development review. The most common are indirect prompt injection (malicious instructions embedded in retrieved documents), guardrail bypass through instruction reformulation, and tool scope escalation through role-playing prompts. None of these require sophisticated technical knowledge — they require systematic enumeration that development teams, focused on functionality, rarely do.

The OWASP LLM Top 10 and the EU AI Act's requirements for high-risk AI systems both demand documented evidence of adversarial testing. A security sign-off without a red-team report is an assertion, not evidence. This engagement produces the evidence.

What makes this different

  • AI-specific, not generic pentest — we do not run a standard web application pentest on your AI infrastructure. Every test case is designed for the specific threat surface of LLM-based agents: prompt manipulation, retrieval exploitation, and tool scope escalation.
  • Indirect injection as a first-class target — most red-team exercises focus on direct user input. We test every content path the agent reads: retrieved documents, tool outputs, email bodies, and CRM records. This is where the most serious production incidents originate.
  • Reusable test suite delivered — the adversarial test cases are a deliverable, not just a report. Your team runs them in CI on every deployment, catching regressions before they reach production.
  • Remediation included, not optional — critical and high findings include a proposed fix, and the engagement budget covers one retest cycle. You leave with a signed sign-off, not a list of open issues.

Client story

Financial services firm, wealth management division — had deployed a customer-facing portfolio summary agent and needed CISO sign-off before expanding from 200 pilot users to the full 8 000-client base. The internal team had reviewed the system prompt and guardrails but had not run adversarial testing.

We ran a 4-week red-team exercise. Critical findings: an indirect prompt injection in PDF investment reports that caused the agent to include raw account numbers in its response; a jailbreak via hypothetical framing that bypassed the financial advice guardrail; and a tool scope escalation that allowed the agent to call the transaction history MCP tool outside its declared read scope. Four high findings and three medium findings. The development team remediated all critical and high findings in 11 days. Retest confirmed all exploits were closed. CISO signed off on the full rollout within three days of the retest report.

Risks we mitigate

  • EU AI Act Article 9 non-compliance — high-risk AI systems require documented risk management measures including adversarial testing evidence. Without a red-team report, enterprises cannot demonstrate compliance with Article 9 requirements and face suspension orders or fines.
  • Indirect injection in retrieval pipelines — the most underestimated attack vector: an attacker who can write to any document store, email system, or CRM the agent reads can hijack agent behaviour for every user who triggers a retrieval of that document.
  • Customer data exfiltration via prompt — an agent with access to customer records and a compromised system prompt can be induced to include customer data in its visible output or to exfiltrate it via outbound tool calls. This is a GDPR breach on every occurrence.

Common questions

We already have a guardrail layer — won't that prevent most of these attacks?
Guardrail layers prevent a significant fraction of attacks but consistently miss some. In our red-team exercises, every guardrail implementation we have tested has had at least two bypass patterns that a systematic adversary would find. The value of red teaming is not finding that guardrails exist — it is finding the specific bypasses that exist in your specific configuration.

Can we run this on a model that isn't Anthropic or OpenAI?
Yes. Our adversarial test methodology is model-agnostic — it targets the agent's behaviour, not the underlying model. We have run red-team exercises on open-source models (Llama, Mistral), Gemini, and fine-tuned enterprise models. The threat categories and test patterns apply regardless of the model.

What happens if critical findings cannot be remediated within the engagement timeline?
Critical findings that cannot be remediated go into the risk register with a documented interim mitigation (typically a kill switch or human-review gate for the affected input path). We do not issue a sign-off for an agent with open critical findings; we issue a conditional sign-off documenting the finding, the interim mitigation, and the required remediation timeline.
